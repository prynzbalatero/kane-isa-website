Are You Overpaying for Security That Doesn't Work?

A uniformed guard at the door isn't always the answer. We break down when guards actually deter crime — and when they don't.

One of the most common misconceptions in the security industry is that visible security equals effective security. A guard at the front desk, a camera above the entrance — these feel reassuring. But feeling secure and being secure are two very different things.

When Guards Actually Work

Uniformed security is genuinely effective in specific contexts: high foot traffic retail environments, events with crowd management needs, and locations where the guard has clearly defined authority and protocols to follow.

The key factor Guards deter opportunistic crime. If your threat is opportunistic — someone who will move on if they see a barrier — a visible guard helps.

When Guards Don't Work

For targeted threats — someone who has specifically identified your business or property — a single uniformed guard provides almost no protection. The same applies to internal theft, which accounts for the majority of losses in retail environments.

In these cases, you're paying for the appearance of security, not the reality of it.

The Real Cost Calculation

A single guard at $35/hr, 8 hours a day, 5 days a week costs over $72,000 per year. Before committing to that expense, every business owner should ask: what specific risk does this guard reduce, and by how much?

Better question to ask "What is my actual threat profile, and what's the most cost-effective way to address it?" ISA can help you answer this in a free consultation.

What Actually Reduces Risk

In most commercial environments, a combination of well-placed cameras with monitored recording, access control systems, and clearly documented SOPs for staff outperforms a single guard at a fraction of the cost.
